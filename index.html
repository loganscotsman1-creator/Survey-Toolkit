Entering angles (D-M-S)

Closed Traverse

Enter each course in order around the figure. The program balances angles, computes latitudes and departures, closure and precision, applies the selected adjustment, and computes coordinates and area.

Missing Side (Traverse)

Leave a value blank or type ? where it is unknown. Closure (ΣLat = 0, ΣDep = 0) supplies two equations, so up to two unknowns can be found:

  • Length and direction of one course
  • Lengths of two courses
  • Directions of two courses (two solutions possible)
  • Length of one course and direction of another (two solutions possible)

Radiation Survey

All points are located by an angle (or direction) and a horizontal distance from one occupied station. The points are joined in the order entered to form the parcel boundary.

Horizontal Curve

Simple circular curve elements and a stakeout table with deflection angles, chords and tangent offsets at every full station of the chosen interval.

Vertical Curve

Equal-tangent parabolic curve. Elevations are computed at the BVC, EVC, PVI, high/low point and every full station of the chosen interval.

Topographic Survey

Load shots from a file or paste them. Supported: CSV / TXT / tab or space delimited (PNEZD, PENZD, PNEZ, PENZ, NEZ, XYZ or any file with a header row), LandXML (CgPoints or surface points) and GeoJSON points with Z.

Help & formulas

Entering angles and directions

  • Angles: 45-30-15, 45°30'15", 45 30 15, 45d30m15s or decimal degrees 45.504167.
  • Bearings: N 45-30-15 E, S12.5W, N 45°30'15" W.
  • Azimuths: a plain angle, measured clockwise from north (0–360°).
  • Stations: 12+34.56 (feet, 100 per station) or 1+234.567 (meters, 1000 per station). Plain numbers also work.
  • Tables accept pasting straight from a spreadsheet (tab or comma separated).

Traverse

  • Latitude = L·cos(Az), Departure = L·sin(Az).
  • Interior angle sum = (n − 2)·180°. The misclosure is distributed equally to each angle.
  • Clockwise traverse: Aznext = Azprev + 180° − ∠. Counter-clockwise: Aznext = Azprev + 180° + ∠.
  • Linear misclosure = √(ΣLat² + ΣDep²); precision = 1 : (perimeter ÷ misclosure).
  • Compass rule: cLat = −ΣLat · L / P, cDep = −ΣDep · L / P.
  • Transit rule: cLat = −ΣLat · |Lat| / Σ|Lat|, cDep = −ΣDep · |Dep| / Σ|Dep|.
  • Area by coordinates: A = ½ |Σ(Ei·Ni+1 − Ei+1·Ni)|, checked by the DMD method.

Horizontal curve

  • Arc definition: R = (base · 180) / (π · D). Chord definition: R = (base / 2) / sin(D/2).
  • T = R·tan(Δ/2), L = base · Δ / D, LC = 2R·sin(Δ/2), E = R(sec(Δ/2) − 1), M = R(1 − cos(Δ/2)).
  • PC = PI − T, PT = PC + L. Deflection to a point l beyond the PC = l·D / (2·base); chord = 2R·sin(deflection).
  • Tangent offsets: x = R·sin θ, y = R(1 − cos θ) with θ = 2 × deflection.

Vertical curve

  • BVC = PVI − L/2, EVC = PVI + L/2, A = g₂ − g₁, K = L / |A|.
  • Elevation x from the BVC: y = yBVC + g₁·x/100 + (A / 200L)·x².
  • High/low point: x = −g₁·L / A (when it falls within the curve).

Topographic survey

  • A Delaunay TIN is built from the shots. Points at identical N/E are kept once and reported.
  • Spikes: each point's elevation is compared with the mean of its TIN neighbours; residuals with a robust z-score above 3.5 are flagged (typical cause: wrong rod height or a shot on an object).
  • Volumes above/below a reference elevation are computed from triangular prisms, split exactly where they cross the reference plane.
  • Contours are traced through the TIN by linear interpolation and can be exported as DXF.

Always check results independently before using them for design, construction or a legal description.
